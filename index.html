The materials beneath everything

Sand and silicates hold up our economies and societies. They also underpin ecological and climate resilience.

Economies

Construction aggregates for buildings, roads, rail and ports. Silica sand for glass and foundry casting. Clays and kaolin for ceramics, tiles and paper.

Societies

Concrete for homes, schools and hospitals. Sand filters for clean water. Glass for packaging, health care and the screens we work on.

Ecological resilience

Sand and sediment in rivers, deltas and coasts. They hold land in place, carry nutrients and keep habitats alive.

Climate resilience

High-purity quartz for solar panels and chips. Sand for coastal defence and beach nourishment. Materials for low-carbon buildings and energy storage.

From impact to business risk

The impacts of sand extraction are now documented and on the global agenda. Due diligence law is about to bring them into supply contracts. That is when they become business risks.

Do you know?

Four questions for procurement, sustainability and continuity teams to answer together.

  1. 1

    How much sand and silicate do your products and projects use? What risks come with where it is sourced?

    About 50 billion tonnes are extracted each year. Only water is extracted more. Up to one-sixth of dredging in 2018–2022 took place in marine protected areas. OECD (2026)

  2. 2

    How dependent are you on them? What would you do if supply stopped?

    One US mine supplies about 70% of high-purity quartz. Hurricane Helene halted it in 2024. OECD (2026)

  3. 3

    What will clients, investors and regulators ask you to show?

    Human rights and environmental due diligence is becoming law. From 2029 the EU's largest companies must apply it to their business partners. Direct suppliers come first. In 2026 the OECD applied its due diligence framework to sand and silicates for the first time. EU Omnibus I (2026); OECD (2026)

  4. 4

    Do you know what you need the evidence for?

    Most teams start from one of two goals.

    • Resilient supply: security of supply, business risk and continuity.
    • Responsible procurement: risk analysis for supply chain due diligence.

    The same evidence serves both.
